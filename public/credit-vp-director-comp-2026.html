01 — Sell Side · Bulge Bracket

Bulge Bracket Banks

Bulge bracket banks remain a common starting point for structured credit careers, and the VP tier spans a meaningful seniority range. We split it into two sub-levels: Junior VP (6–8 years) and Senior VP (9–12 years), which correspond roughly to VP and SVP/ED titles depending on the bank. Roughly one third of bonus is deferred into restricted stock vesting over 3–5 years.

Senior VP / ED upper band (£530k bonus) reflects top-of-pool outcomes in strong years at the largest banks — not a median result. Total comp P10–P90 range is £500k–£775k; most Senior VPs sit in the £520k–£610k interquartile range.

  • Base is higher than most buy-side equivalents at the same seniority — banks use it as a fixed-cost retention lever in a way that credit funds typically don't
  • Roughly one third of bonus is paid as restricted stock vesting over 3–5 years; this deferred component is often the single biggest friction point for candidates considering a buy-side move, as unvested stock is forfeited on departure
  • Bonus is discretionary and pool-allocated
  • On a pure cash basis Junior VP at a bulge bracket (£300k–£525k) is competitive with VP at an alt credit fund; Senior VP (£475k–£750k) is ahead of most buy-side equivalents on cash alone — but the deferred stock and absence of carry change the long-term picture materially
02 — Alternative Credit Fund ($5–25bn)

VP & Director at Alt. Credit Funds Director figures exclude Managing Directors

Data covers $10–50bn alternative credit platforms — not megafunds, where comp at this level is meaningfully higher. Compensation at this level has three components:

  • Base salary — modest relative to total compensation; provides income stability but is not the primary retention lever
  • Bonus (cash & deferred) — the dominant component, typically 100–150% of base at VP level and more at Director; a portion is deferred into fund shares vesting over 3–5 years
  • Carry — begins to appear at VP level, becomes a meaningful part of the package at Director; the size and quality of carry allocation is often the central negotiating point at this tier

Director upper band reflects top-of-range outcomes at well-performing funds — not a typical result. The majority of Directors sit in the £340k–£500k range (P10–Q3). The P90 of £735k is attainable but uncommon and generally requires strong fund performance, long tenure, and meaningful deal involvement. Total comp ranges are computed per-print; carry not included.

  • Carry at Junior VP level is typically very low — allocations exist but are small, so the comp table is close to total compensation for this cohort
  • The deferred fund shares component only triggers above the £350k bonus threshold — a Director on a £450k bonus will typically receive around £25k–£50k in fund shares (20–30% of the slice above £350k); these vest over 3–5 years and are forfeited on departure, making them a meaningful retention tool alongside carry
  • Fund shares vest into an employee co-investment vehicle — distinct from the main fund but investing alongside it. In strong vintages the value at vest exceeds grant value; in weaker ones it does not
  • At Director level carry typically begins, though allocations vary significantly by fund size and vintage; this is the critical variable to interrogate in any offer
  • Bad leaver clauses become a constraint from Director upwards — unvested and in some cases vested carry can be subject to haircuts on departure, making lateral moves more costly than they appear on paper
  • Co-investment rights typically open at Director level alongside carry — meaningful additional upside, but requires tying up personal capital (though at fund leverage terms, which is structurally favourable)
