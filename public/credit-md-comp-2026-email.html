  • Base is the least negotiable component — most funds treat it as a fixed cost and won't flex it materially at MD level
  • Candidates coming from investment banking often push back on the base level — IBs pay considerably better base salaries at MD level, occasionally $400–600k for senior MDs. In practice the push back is rarely a dealbreaker and base is not a lever funds will move on
  • Nobody at this level is choosing a fund based on base — the important bit is carry, fund shares, and co-invest

  • Vesting runs 3–5 years, cliff or graded; in strong vintages the value at vest exceeds grant value materially
  • Introduces downside in weaker vintages and is illiquid until vesting

2 funds stacked (typical) — min $1.2m · median $4.5m · max $5.4m

Expected value at time of grant; realised over 5–10 years. Whiskers show P10–P90. Box spans Q1–Q3. Line = median.

Note: Valuing carry is inherently difficult and highly subjective — figures reflect expected value at time of grant, not guaranteed outcomes. Actual realisation depends on fund performance, vintage timing, hurdle rates, and LP cashflow profiles. Treat these numbers as directional, not contractual.

  • Allocation at Senior MD level is 1–1.5 points of the carry pool per fund
  • Distribution timing depends on waterfall structure: deal-by-deal (American) waterfalls pay progressively; whole-fund (European) waterfalls defer all carry to end-of-fund
  • Most Senior MDs sit across 2 fund vintages simultaneously; typical stack is $1.2–5.4m expected unvested carry
  • When comparing offers, the points allocation is only part of the picture — vintage age, deployment progress, hurdle status, and LP base all affect real expected value
  • A 1.5pt allocation in a fund 18 months into deployment is worth considerably more than 1.5pts in a fund still in early capital calls

04 — Co-Invest & GP Commitment

Co-Invest & GP Commitment

Leverage at fund terms

  • The most underappreciated component — and the one candidates least often negotiate
  • Core mechanic: GP commitment and co-invest capital goes into the fund itself, which operates with fund-level leverage. This capital typically attracts no management fee and no carry — meaning you participate in the same returns as LPs but keep the full upside. Some firms also provide financing against committed capital
  • At Senior MD level, co-invest is usually part of the package — either mandatory GP commitment or optional deal-by-deal participation
  • Larger platforms tend toward mandatory commitment; smaller or newer funds offer more flexibility
  • Over a 10-year career with consistent co-invest access and solid fund performance, the compounding effect can be material — and the returns are more predictable in timing than carry
