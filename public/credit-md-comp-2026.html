Note: this is relevant only to front office investment professionals (deal sourcing, execution, and portfolio management) at specialist private market (mainly credit) funds in the $10–50bn AUM segment. It does not cover IR, operations, or risk functions. Based on 27 data points.

  • Base is the least negotiable component — most funds treat it as a fixed cost and won't flex it materially at MD level
  • Candidates coming from investment banking often push back on the base level — IBs pay considerably better base salaries at MD level, occasionally $400–600k for senior MDs. In practice the push back is rarely a dealbreaker and base is not a lever funds will move on
  • Nobody at this level is choosing a fund based on base — the important bit is carry, fund shares, and co-invest

Expected value at time of grant; realised over 5–10 years. Distribution timing depends on waterfall structure — deal-by-deal (American) waterfalls often pay pre-harvest; whole-fund (European) waterfalls pay at the end. Whiskers show P10–P90. Box spans Q1–Q3. Line = median.

Note: Valuing carry is inherently difficult and highly subjective — the figures above reflect expected value at time of grant, not guaranteed outcomes. Actual realisation depends on fund performance, vintage timing, hurdle rates, and LP cashflow profiles. Values assume one point equals 1% of the carry pool, lifetime fund profits of roughly 40% of commitments and a 15% carry rate (pool ≈ 6% of fund size), undiscounted. Treat these numbers as directional, not contractual.

Figures presented as aggregated statistics to protect anonymity.

  • Allocation at Senior MD level is 1–1.5 points of the carry pool per fund (individual fund vehicles typically $1bn–$3bn at this manager scale)
  • Distribution timing depends on waterfall structure: deal-by-deal (American) waterfalls often pay carry progressively as income and principal recoveries land; whole-fund (European) waterfalls defer all carry to end-of-fund — common at larger, more institutional credit managers
  • Most Senior MDs sit across 2 fund vintages simultaneously; per-fund expected unvested carry is $0.6–2.7m, giving a typical stack of $1.2–5.4m
  • When comparing offers, the points allocation is only part of the picture — vintage age, deployment progress, hurdle status, and LP base all affect the real expected value

04 — Co-Invest & GP Commitment

Co-Invest & GP Commitment

Leverage at fund terms
  • The most underappreciated component — and the one candidates least often negotiate
  • Core mechanic: GP commitment and co-invest capital goes into the fund itself, which operates with fund-level leverage. The structural advantage is that this capital typically attracts no management fee and no carry — meaning you participate in the same returns as LPs but keep the full upside. Some firms also provide financing against committed capital. For a direct lending fund generating high single to low double digit net returns (opportunistic credit higher), this is a structurally attractive position
  • At Senior MD level, co-invest is usually part of the package — either mandatory GP commitment (signals conviction to LPs) or optional deal-by-deal participation
  • Larger platforms tend toward mandatory commitment; smaller or newer funds offer more flexibility
  • Over a 10-year career with consistent co-invest access and solid fund performance, the compounding effect can be material — and the returns are more predictable in timing than carry, which is subject to waterfall structure and fund lifecycle
