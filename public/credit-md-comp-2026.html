  • Base is the least negotiable component — most funds treat it as a fixed cost and won't flex it materially at MD level
  • Candidates coming from investment banking often push back on the base level — IBs pay considerably better base salaries at MD level, occasionally $400–600k for senior MDs. In practice the push back is rarely a dealbreaker and base is not a lever funds will move on
  • Nobody at this level is choosing a fund based on base — the important bit is carry, fund shares, and co-invest

  • A number of credit funds pay the deferred portion as shares in an employee-managed fund — a separate vehicle from the main fund, run specifically for staff investment
  • Vesting runs 3–5 years, cliff or graded; in strong vintages the value at vest exceeds grant value materially
  • Introduces downside in weaker vintages and is illiquid until vesting
  • Unvested fund shares are forfeited on departure, making this a meaningful retention mechanism

Actual realisation depends on fund performance, vintage timing, hurdle rates, and LP cashflow profiles. Treat these numbers as directional, not contractual.

Figures amended with ±1–8% at random to ensure full anonymity.

  • Allocation at Senior MD level is 1–1.5 points of the carry pool per fund (individual fund vehicles typically $500m–$3bn at this manager scale)
  • Usually, credit carry pays pre-harvest, distributed progressively over the fund's life as income and principal recoveries land
  • Most Senior MDs sit across 2 fund vintages simultaneously; per-fund expected unvested carry is $1.5–3m, giving a typical stack of $3–6m — the upper end ($7.5–10m) requires 3 funds stacked and all performing well above hurdle
  • When comparing offers, the points allocation is only part of the picture — vintage age, deployment progress, hurdle status, and LP base all affect the real expected value
    • This is the crux that makes or breaks a deal, and something we are happy to discuss in detail.

  • Hypothetical Example: a Senior MD was paid 4% of a $25m+ P&L deal that was very clearly the MD's own origination — at the full discretion of the partner/CIO, not negotiated at hire
  • Only a small number of funds offer this. It will rarely appear in a contract; it is almost always an informal understanding between the MD and the partner/CIO
  • Most funds deliberately avoid it as it can skew investment behaviour towards fee-generating risk rather than portfolio-optimal risk
  • Where it exists, the fee is paid as a cash bonus in the year following deal realisation, not the deal year itself
06 — Co-Invest & GP Commitment

Co-Invest & GP Commitment

Leverage at fund terms
  • The most underappreciated component — and the one candidates least often negotiate
  • Core mechanic: invest personal capital alongside the fund at the same leverage terms the fund secures from its lenders; for a fund generating mid-teens net returns at 100–150bps over SOFR, that's structurally very attractive access
  • At Senior MD level, co-invest is usually part of the package — either mandatory GP commitment (signals conviction to LPs) or optional deal-by-deal participation
  • Larger platforms tend toward mandatory commitment; smaller or newer funds offer more flexibility
  • Over a 10-year career with consistent co-invest access, this compounds into the single most valuable wealth accumulation component — more than carry, and more predictable than deal P&L fees
